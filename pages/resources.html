Help

Hotlines and support.

If you're in crisis, or you've decided you want some support — these are confidential, free or low-cost, and don't require you to commit to anything.

!

If you're having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, or are in immediate danger:

988 — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US, call or text, 24/7). Free, confidential.

911 for any acute medical emergency, including severe vomiting/dehydration from CHS that you can't keep ahead of at home.

Specifically for substance use

SAMHSA National Helpline

1-800-662-HELP (4357) — Free, confidential, 24/7. Treatment referrals and information for substance use and mental health. They don't lecture, they don't moralize, they don't pressure. They give you options near you.

samhsa.gov/find-help/national-helpline

Marijuana Anonymous

A peer-support program modeled on AA. Free. Meetings worldwide, plus phone and video. No requirement to commit to a religious framework. Some people find this enormously useful; others find it doesn't fit. No commitment to find out.

marijuana-anonymous.org

SMART Recovery

A secular, science-based alternative to 12-step programs. CBT-oriented. Online and in-person meetings, plus a discussion forum. Many people who don't connect with the 12-step approach do connect with this.

smartrecovery.org

Mental health

NAMI HelpLine

1-800-950-NAMI (6264) — Free information, support, and referrals for any mental health concern. Useful if you're not sure what kind of help you need.

nami.org/help

Crisis Text Line

Text HOME to 741741 (US/Canada). Trained crisis counselors via text. Useful if calling is hard.

crisistextline.org

For people who don't use cannabis but love someone who does

Al-Anon / Nar-Anon family groups

These programs are for the family members of people with substance use problems. The principles are the same regardless of which substance. Free meetings online and in-person.

al-anon.org · nar-anon.org

If you're worried about someone, the most evidence-based thing you can do is the opposite of confrontation: stay connected, express concern without ultimatums, and let them know you'll be there whether or not they're using. The CRAFT (Community Reinforcement and Family Training) approach has the strongest evidence base for family members and is more effective than traditional intervention models.

If you're a clinician looking to share this site with a patient: the index page (index.html) is the intended landing point. All pages are static, mobile-friendly, and contain no tracking. Patients can use the tools without anything being stored or transmitted.
