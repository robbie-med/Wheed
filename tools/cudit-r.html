Cannabis Use Disorder Identification Test (Revised)

The CUDIT-R is the validated 8-question screening tool used in clinical research for hazardous cannabis use and possible cannabis use disorder (Adamson et al., 2010). Answer about the past 6 months. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere — calculations happen in your browser.

What this gives you: a number between 0 and 32, with cutoffs from the validation study. What this doesn't give you: a diagnosis. Only a clinician can diagnose cannabis use disorder.

1. How often do you use cannabis?
2. How many hours were you "stoned" on a typical day when you had been using cannabis?
3. How often during the past 6 months did you find that you were not able to stop using cannabis once you had started?
4. How often during the past 6 months did you fail to do what was normally expected of you because of using cannabis?
5. How often in the past 6 months have you devoted a great deal of your time to getting, using, or recovering from cannabis?
6. How often in the past 6 months have you had a problem with your memory or concentration after using cannabis?
7. How often do you use cannabis in situations that could be physically hazardous, such as driving, operating machinery, or caring for children?
8. Have you ever thought about cutting down, or stopping, your use of cannabis?

How to read this

The CUDIT-R was validated against DSM-IV cannabis abuse and dependence criteria in Adamson et al., Drug and Alcohol Dependence 2010. Cutoffs from that validation:

ScoreInterpretation
0–7Low risk. Use, if any, is unlikely to be problematic.
8–11Hazardous use. In the original Adamson 2010 validation sample, the 8-point cutoff had sensitivity ~91% and specificity ~90% for hazardous cannabis use — those figures are specific to that population and may not generalize to other settings.
12+Possible cannabis use disorder. A clinical evaluation is reasonable.

The CUDIT-R is a screening tool, not a diagnostic instrument. A high score does not mean you have CUD; it means the questionnaire matches the pattern often seen in people who do. A low score doesn't rule out problems either — particularly if your use is recent or you've had a low-use stretch in the past 6 months.

What to do with a result you didn't expect

If you scored higher than you expected, two reasonable next steps:

  1. Read the withdrawal, tolerance, and quitting pages. Many "high CUDIT-R" patterns can be substantially improved without formal treatment.
  2. Bring the result to a clinician you trust. Most clinicians under 50 are not anti-cannabis; they're trying to figure out what's making your week harder. A CUDIT-R score gives the conversation a number to work with.

If you scored lower than you expected, that's reassuring data — but the CUDIT-R doesn't measure CHS risk, lung effects, or interactions with other parts of your life. Low CUDIT-R + a real problem in your life can both be true.

Reference

Adamson SJ, Kay-Lambkin FJ, Baker AL, Lewin TJ, Thornton L, Kelly BJ, Sellman JD. An improved brief measure of cannabis misuse: the Cannabis Use Disorder Identification Test-Revised (CUDIT-R). Drug and Alcohol Dependence. 2010;110(1-2):137–143.
