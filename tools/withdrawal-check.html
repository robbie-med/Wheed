Cannabis withdrawal symptom check

Adapted from the Cannabis Withdrawal Scale (Allsop et al., 2012) and the DSM-5 withdrawal criteria. Rate each symptom for how it's been for you over the past 24 hours. Nothing is stored or sent.

This is most informative if you've recently stopped or significantly cut down on cannabis, or if you're considering doing so and want a baseline. You can repeat it daily — the curve (each day's score over time) is often more useful than any single number.

For each symptom: 0 = not at all · 1 = mild · 2 = moderate · 3 = severe.

How to read this

The Cannabis Withdrawal Scale was validated by Allsop et al. (PLoS ONE 2012) for measuring withdrawal severity in research and clinical contexts. This version is a simplified, 0–3-scored adaptation for self-use. It captures the symptom set that's diagnostic for cannabis withdrawal under DSM-5-TR.

Total scorePattern
0–8Minimal. Few or no withdrawal-pattern symptoms above background.
9–16Mild withdrawal pattern.
17–28Moderate withdrawal pattern — clinically significant.
29+Severe withdrawal pattern.

For interpretation, the timing relative to your last use matters as much as the number:

  • Day 1: Symptoms are usually mild, often missed entirely. A high score on day 1 suggests heavy use and likely a hard several days ahead.
  • Days 2–6 (peak): This is when scores are highest. A moderate-to-severe score here is the expected pattern in heavy users — not a sign of danger, but a signal to ride it out and not return to use.
  • Week 2: Scores should be dropping. If they aren't, look at sleep and mood — those linger longest.
  • Week 3+: Most symptoms should be down significantly. Persistent sleep disturbance is common but tractable.

If your score is high

This is the expected curve, not a problem with you. Cannabis withdrawal is uncomfortable and not dangerous. You don't need medical care for it unless symptoms are severe, scary, or include features that don't fit (like persistent vomiting that doesn't track with the timeline — that could be CHS rather than straightforward withdrawal).

Concrete things that help, from the quitting page: sleep planning, exercise, hydration, not stacking alcohol or sedatives, and telling someone you're going through it.

If your score is low but you're sure you're in withdrawal

A few possibilities:

  • It's still too early. Withdrawal peaks at day 2–6 for most heavy users.
  • You weren't a heavy enough user for severe withdrawal. (Good news.)
  • You're in the protracted phase — weeks in, with low-grade mood or sleep changes only. This is common and resolves slowly.
  • What you're experiencing is something else — your underlying mental health, sleep disorder, or something unrelated to cannabis.

Reference

Allsop DJ, Norberg MM, Copeland J, Fu S, Budney AJ. The Cannabis Withdrawal Scale development: patterns and predictors of cannabis withdrawal and distress. PLoS ONE. 2012;7(9):e44864.
